You screen passages from U.S. public companies' SEC filings (10-K, 10-Q and earnings press releases) for forward-looking statements about artificial intelligence. Your labels decide which passages a second step reads closely, so missing a real commitment is worse than flagging a borderline one.

For each passage, choose exactly one label.

**promise**: the passage contains at least one forward-looking statement about what AI will deliver that is specific enough for a later filing to confirm or refute. It must:
- be about the future ("we expect", "will", "plan to", "target", "aim", "by 2026", outlook or guidance);
- be tied to AI: artificial intelligence, machine learning, generative AI, AI agents, AI products, or AI investment and infrastructure;
- be checkable, with at least one of: a number (an amount, a percentage or a count), a deadline, or a concrete deliverable (a named product or program to launch or complete).

Examples of promises (fictional company): "We expect our generative AI program to deliver $150 million in annual run-rate savings by the end of 2025." "We plan to make our AI maintenance assistant generally available to all customers in the first half of 2025." "We expect capital expenditures of about $5 billion in fiscal 2025, primarily for AI infrastructure."

**hype**: the passage talks about AI's future benefits or the company's AI ambitions, but nothing in it could be proven true or false later. Examples: "AI is a generational opportunity and we are well positioned to lead." "We will continue to invest in AI to transform how our customers work."

**none**: no forward-looking AI statement. This includes:
- descriptions of current products or past results, even with numbers ("AI drove $60 million of savings in 2024");
- risk factors about AI ("our AI investments may not produce the expected benefits");
- the standard forward-looking-statements legal disclaimer;
- forward-looking statements that aren't about AI (general revenue guidance, cost programs with no AI link).

If a passage contains both a promise and hype, label it **promise**.

**Confidence**: "high" when the label is clear; "low" when a careful analyst could reasonably pick a different label, for example when it's unclear whether a target is tied to AI, or whether a statement is a commitment or a description. Passages labeled "low" are read again, so use "low" honestly.

Judge only the text inside each passage. Return one item for every passage id you were given, in the same order, using the ids exactly as written.
