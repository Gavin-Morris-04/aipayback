Methodology

How a sentence in a filing becomes a status

How we find AI promises, check them against later filings, measure our own accuracy, and what we don't do.

Statuses

{{ chip("Delivered") }}

A later filing reports an outcome that meets the target (within the tolerance in our rubric) by the deadline.

{{ chip("On track") }}

The deadline hasn't passed and a later filing reports a measurable progress figure. Reaffirmation alone isn't enough.

{{ chip("No evidence yet") }}

Nothing in the later filings confirms or refutes it.

{{ chip("Walked back") }}

A later filing lowers, delays or drops the commitment. "Silent" walk-backs (the deadline passed and later filings stopped mentioning it) are flagged separately and need stricter conditions.

{{ chip(PENDING) }}

Our checks didn't reach the confidence we require. We show the verified quote only.

How it works

  1. Sources. 10-K and 10-Q filings and earnings releases (8-K Item 2.02) from SEC EDGAR, plus companies' XBRL financial data. Promises come from filings dated 2024–2025; evidence from every later filing.
  2. Finding promises. A keyword filter, then a language model, picks out forward-looking AI statements. Every quote is checked by code to appear word for word in the filing.
  3. Checking them. Code searches the company's later filings and computes the relevant reported figures. A model judges the status from that material only. Uncertain cases and every "Walked back" get a second, independent check, and a negative status is published only when both checks agree.
  4. Explaining. A short explanation cites the exact sentences it relies on, and code verifies every citation against the source.
  5. Neutral wording. Generated text is checked against a list of judgment words. We describe what filings report; we don't judge management.

Limitations

  • SEC filings only: no earnings-call transcripts or news, so promises made only on calls aren't covered.
  • Promises from late 2025 have a short evidence window; many will read "No evidence yet" for a while.
  • Companies define their own metrics ("run-rate savings"); when a filing reports progress on its own definition, we report it as stated.
  • Statuses describe what filings report. They aren't judgments of management, and nothing here is investment or legal advice.

Errors

Email {{ site.contact_email }}. An item with an open report is held as "Status pending review" until we recheck it, and corrections are listed in the next newsletter.
